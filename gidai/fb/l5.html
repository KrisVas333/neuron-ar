AT A GLANCE — THE 1-MINUTE VERSION

🎯 WHAT THIS LESSON WANTS FROM YOU: 

teach one word — PSEUDOCODE (a plan for a program in human words, symbols or pictures — the younglings just says plan) · also: BUG · older class also: ALGORITHM · 

one technique — plan → predict → code → check — one plan card for each block, read out loud · 

one belief — "If I plan it well, my robot does exactly what I meant." If the kids leave with those three, you won.


"After today I can plan before I code."  (the Promise — Slide 2, checked on Slide 20)


WHAT THE KID WALKS OUT WITH: A flight plan their crew wrote for a forest another crew built, and a plane that flew it onto the landing spot.

WHAT THE PARENT HEARS AT HOME: "We built the future's first plane, wrote its flight plan first, then coded it — and it landed on the spot in another team's forest."

✅ SUCCESS: within 1–2 weeks the kid plans a task out loud in steps ("first… then… then…") before starting it  ·  ❌ FAIL: at Slide 20 nobody can say why we planned before coding

SLIDE 6Pick your minifigure blindly (LEGO 3 · Collaboration norms)90: 2 · 60: 2 min
  • Crews of 4. Each kid picks a minifigure without looking. The colour is their job for the whole lesson, starting with the game on the next slide:
  • 🔵 Pilot — in the game is the plane; later the only one on the tablet · 🔴 Navigator — reads the plan, one card at a time (younglings: says the arrow) · 🟢 Builder — lays the cards; later builds the plane and the forest · 🟡 Checker — points at each card and shouts STOP before a tree.
  • LEGO prints matching colour icons in the building instructions, so the same colour also tells each kid which part they build.
  • Norms, in one breath: share the materials, work together, ask each other for help.

SLIDE 7GAME: Human Plane (ours — not in LEGO's lesson)90: 5 · 60: 5 min
  • Set up (per crew, on the floor): START = a book · 2–3 chairs = the trees · LANDING SPOT = the sheet with the big H, a few steps away. Per crew: 9 arrow cards (5 ↑ · 2 ↰ · 2 ↱). No tape: one step = one heel-to-toe step.
  • 🟢 Builder lays the arrow cards in a row: ↑ one step · ↰ turn left · ↱ turn right. 🔴 Navigator reads ONE card out loud. 🔵 Pilot (arms out — the plane) does exactly that card, nothing more. 🟡 Checker points at the card being read and shouts STOP before a chair.
  • One round only — the plan has a bug. Hand each crew a plan with one TURN card missing. The plane walks into a chair. ❓ "Which card is missing?" Then name it: "A mistake in a plan is called a BUG. Finding it and fixing it is called DEBUGGING." Put the card in, fly again, land.
  • STOP rule: the plane says "STOP" before it touches a chair or a wall, and the crew fixes the plan.
  • Younglings: arrow cards only. This is the most important activity of their lesson — keep it even if something else gets shorter.
SLIDE 8You just wrote pseudocode! (LEGO 1 + 2 · Introduction + Context)90: 3 · 60: 2 min
  • Name what they just did — the word lands on the game, not on nothing. ❓ "What did the plane need to know before it walked?" Take: the way · what's in the way.
  • Point at the word box: "Your cards were a plan. A plan for a program, in words, symbols or pictures, is called PSEUDOCODE. There are no set rules."
  • The picture is a map app planning a car journey — same idea on the ground.
  • Younglings: say plan. The word pseudocode is optional — use it only if they are enjoying the long word.
SLIDE 9Today's plan90: 1 · 60: 1 min
  • Four steps: 1 build the future's first plane → 2 predict the flight → 3 run the code: were we right? → 4 fly through another crew's forest.
  • Tablets stay face down until Slide 12.
SLIDE 10BIG BUILD: the future's first plane (LEGO 4 · Build together)90: 9 · 60: 11 min
  • "Building instructions C1, page 24. Seven minutes!" Use the timer.
  • Finished early? Explore the moves the plane can make — straight · left · right · back up — and help another crew finish.
  • Circulate constantly. A crew still building at Slide 11 cannot predict.
  • Younglings: give them 11 minutes.
SLIDE 11Predict: where will the plane stop? (LEGO 5)90: 5 · 60: 4 min
  • Tablets still face down. All eyes on the plan, not on a screen.
  • 1 Put the plane on ONE start spot at the table edge, nose pointing forward. 2 Read the plan together: fly straight ahead · turn left · fly straight · turn right · fly straight. 3 Put ONE minifigure on the table exactly where you think the plane's nose will stop.
  • Right or wrong is fine — we find out on Slide 13. Don't move the plane or the minifigure until then.
  • Bigger kids — 1–2 minutes on ALGORITHM (the word on the slide points at the plan): ❓ "Who knows what an algorithm is?" Take guesses, then: "An algorithm is a list of steps, in the right order, that gets something done — like a recipe, or getting dressed in the morning. This plan is an algorithm for the plane." ❓ "What happens if we swap step 2 and step 4?"
  • Younglings: skip the word algorithm.

SLIDE 12CONNECT: the handshake90: 3 · 60: 3 min
  • The plane and the minifigure stay exactly where the crew put them.
  • Pair YOUR hub on the projector first. Double Motor only today.
  • If a hub will not pair: off and on once, then move the crew onto a working hub. Debug after class.
SLIDE 13Try it! (LEGO 6)90: 4 · 60: 4 min
  • Bigger kids: code.legoeducation.com → PIN 1303 → the starter code (word blocks) is ready → connect → ▶ Play.
  • Younglings: PIN 1303 opens word blocks only. Instead: New project → Icon blocks → blank canvas. Build the pilot's plan on the projector with icon blocks (forward · turn left · forward · turn right · forward); crews copy it block by block, then ▶.
  • First one calibration flight: ❓ "How many plan cards long is one fly-straight on our table?" A plan card is the ruler.
  • Then: ❓ "Did the plane stop at your minifigure?" Compare the blocks on screen with the plan.
SLIDE 14Check in (LEGO 7 · Explain)90: 5 · 60: 2 min
  • ❓ "What was the SAME in the plan and the code? What was DIFFERENT?" Look for: the blocks set direction and distance; the order of the blocks is the order of the plan.
  • ❓ "When is a plan helpful?" Look for: quick to make · other people can read it · it helps you plan and remember what the program does.
  • Younglings: one question only — "Did the plane do what the plan said?"
⚠️ ⤷ TRANSITION INTO SLIDE 15 — draft, replace with Kris's wording "LEGO's plan worked. Now YOU build a forest — but someone else has to fly through it."
SLIDE 15Build a forest! (LEGO 8 · Elaborate, part 1)90: 4 · 60: 2 min
  • Each crew builds a forest on its own table, for ANOTHER crew: tall trees and a power line (max 5 bricks each), a START spot, and a landing spot marked with one minifigure.
  • It must be possible. The builders must be able to point out a way through. They do NOT write a plan for their own forest.
  • Younglings: ONE tree per crew; help them place the landing spot.
SLIDE 16Swap forests! (LEGO 8 · Elaborate, part 2)90: 12 · 60: 9 min
  • Step 1 Move to the next table (clockwise). Step 2 Write the plan — Navigator writes, Checker checks each step against the forest. Step 3 Pilot codes it. Step 4 Fly it — land on the minifigure.
  • Crash? "That's a bug. Fix the PLAN first, then the code." Jobs rotate one seat clockwise after every flight.
  • Why a stranger's forest: you can't fly on memory — only the plan gets you through. That is the whole point of pseudocode.
  • Younglings: arrow cards, the whole crew plans together, icon blocks with the mentor.
  • Running late? Cut Slide 17, never 20–22.
SLIDE 17Harder forest! (ours — LEGO's extension as a game) 🟡 BIGGER KIDS ONLY90: 10 · 60: skip min
  • Each crew adds ONE power line (a wall the plane cannot cross) to the forest it just flew. Then all crews move one more table: new forest, new plan.
  • Lands: both crews cheer. Crashes: find the bug together. "A good plan works for other people too."
  • Younglings: skips this slide.
SLIDE 18Show us your flight! (LEGO 9 · Share)90: 6 · 60: 2 min
  • Each crew 60 s, standing at the forest it flew: read the plan out loud, then fly it. The forest's builders judge: landed on the spot? ✅
  • Check understanding: can they say pseudocode is written for PEOPLE to read? Did their plan get the plane onto the landing spot?
  • Younglings: no presentation to the room. Crew by crew: "Show me your plan, then fly it."
SLIDE 19Flight School Quiz (ours — bigger kids · LEGO 10 · Evaluate) 🟡 BIGGER KIDS ONLY90: 7 · 60: skip min
  • Open the quiz on the projector: Flight School Quiz. 10 questions; kids answer with 1–4 fingers.
  • Space shows the answer, → goes on. Read the gold "Mentor reads" line after every answer — that sentence is the teaching.
  • No scores and no names. It is a check for you: if most fingers are wrong on questions 1, 6 or 10, go back to slide 8 next lesson.
SLIDE 20You did it, Future Builders!90: 1 · 60: 1 min
  • "After today I can plan before I code. TRUE?" Thumbs up.
  • Then one kid: ❓ "Why did we plan BEFORE coding?" That answer is the lesson — wait for it.
SLIDE 21The Tomorrow Map — our first thing on the map: the plane90: 1 · 60: 1 min
  • "Our first thing on the map: THE PLANE! Now we can fly anywhere on it. Next time, the map shows us where to fly next…" The plane is the first piece of the future we built — it goes on the blank map. The next ??? stays.
  • From now on the map opens and closes every lesson: each lesson adds one new thing we built. Don't explain the next ???.
SLIDE 22Every brick goes home! (LEGO 11 · Clean up)90: 5 · 60: 5 min
  • Forests come down first. Then invoke ORDER: "Every colour back to its home. Can we make it in 3 minutes?" Sort by colour → close the box → high five.
  • Plan cards go back in their envelope, NOT into the box. Hubs and motors back charged.

About 2 minutes for you, and about 90 seconds for one volunteer kid. Do both while the lesson is still fresh — not that evening.

Never your name or a child's name, whatever the agent tells you. The L and the number are the only part that matters: they link your debrief to your volunteer's, so the two get read side by side.
